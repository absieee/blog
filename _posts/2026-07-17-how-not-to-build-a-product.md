What comes to mind when you think of Product Management in the current age?

It was never about creating features just because you can or even about how much you can "get done" in a day. 

See, from what I have seen, there is no "productivity" problem to solve in Product Management.

There is, however, a learning problem. 

PMs nowadays simply can not comprehend or appreciate what AI is capable of, and are lost on how to augment agents to transform their skillset instead of being consumed by the wave.

In fact, many still flinch when asked about an API or why the latency in their AI product is so high, and immediately panic DM their engineers to answer such questions.

Many Product professionals still believe that asking AI to rewrite an email or JIRA ticket is the peak of what this technology can do. Such PMs are still living in the past - they are the PMs who still choose to spend all days in back to back meetings explaining the same thing to the same stakeholder for the 19th time, and spend the evenings catching up on what action items they were assigned so they can call it "a busy day".
These are the PMs who will push engineers to be accountable for a delivery time while side-stepping  

They don't have the curiosity, urgency or luxury (of time) to truly lean into the AI wave so instead, they remain skeptical and would rather spend weeks and months performing manual admin tasks that agents can do in a matter of seconds.

However, I feel no empathy or sympathy for such PMs, they are **not the ones at fault**.

The truth is despite the number of "AI PM" roles in circulation, the industry is just not ready for those who have made the effort to break out of their "product" shell and provide the value of 10 while being paid a salary of 1.

No corporate company is ready for the PM who can run multiple agents on a daily basis to automate the common admin tasks of writing stories, grooming backlogs, updating roadmaps and creating slide decks. 
Why? 
Because traditionally this role has belonged in a Scrum Master or Delivery Manager's job description. Besides, how can a PM with no CSM, PSM or SASM certification outperform a Scrum Master that has them all, right? 
Wrong.

No engineering department of a company believes a PM can spot the gap in architectural decisions, question design systems or why the user experience simply does not make sense. Why? Because then how will the overpaid Solution Architects and UX/UI Designers be justified? Of course, I am not saying such titles no longer matter, I am just saying PMs who have managed to successfully augment themselves with AI now have a seat at the architectural table too. So don't leave them out.

This one is the biggest one that a reader may roll their eyes on - no one wants the PM who can become a domain expert overnight on any complex topic. Why? If the PM becomes the knowledgable one, then what will the non-technical "SMEs" who have 10-20 years of experience in a specific industry bring to the table? The truth is it's their network that is the bigger leverage, not the knowledge. All it takes is running a simple ELI5 me [insert topic name here] to any LLM and a high IQ PM will easily be able to grasp what they need to without needing to do more than a couple 1-hour KT sessions with SMEs.

What i describe above will resonate with the PM that is regularly augmenting themselves while on the other hand, there will be the aggresive nay-sayers who thinks "PMs are no longer needed at all".

News flash: this post is meant to cater to neither.

It is meant to be read as a wake-up call. A PSA, if you will.
